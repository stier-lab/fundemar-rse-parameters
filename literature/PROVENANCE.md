## Aldo's follow-up on reproductive size

His 19 October 2026, 17:16:56 UTC reply (Gmail message `1a1552ab628cda0a`) recalled “Son & Lang” and about 10–15 cm at first reproductive structures. Verified the intended source as Soong K and Lang JC (1992), *Reproductive integration in reef corals*, Biological Bulletin 183:418–431, DOI https://doi.org/10.2307/1542018. Used the correctly identified existing local record `p001204`, not duplicate catalogue records carrying a different DOI or journal. Copied the unchanged PDF into `pdfs/data_studies/Soong_Lang_1992_reproductive_integration_reef_corals.pdf` for a self-contained project library; no original or shared-store PDF was edited.

Source checks: p. 420 describes Panama sampling, reproductive-condition assays, area measurement, and the operational puberty-size criterion (the smallest colony in the first consecutive size-ranked group of 20 with at least 90% fertile). Table VI, p. 425, reports increasing fertility with size, including 88% for A. palmata above 4,000 cm² (n = 33). Table VII, p. 426, reports a smallest fertile A. palmata colony of 16 × 8 cm and estimated puberty size of 1,600 cm². The A. palmata area measure sums living branch and base areas, unlike the synthesis's live planar area. These observations do not establish a universal 10–15 cm cutoff, a fertility assignment for our classes, successful spawning/fertilization/recruitment, or a before–after reproductive response to shrinkage. This is contextual evidence, not a new demographic study or a new model parameter.
