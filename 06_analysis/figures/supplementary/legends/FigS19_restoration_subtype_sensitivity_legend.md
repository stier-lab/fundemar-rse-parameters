# Fig. S5 (repository asset FigS19): population-source descriptions

(a) Observed interval survival and (b) relative live-area growth rate (yr⁻¹). The same 8 observed origin/handling groups appear in both panels, with naturally occurring fragments first and study names in parentheses. Open circles are study–region summaries, with point area scaled to record counts; filled diamonds, offset above each row for visibility, are record-weighted subtype means. Blue diamonds denote survival and orange diamonds growth, not population origin. Survival percentages average binary colony-interval fates. The legacy per-record power transformation leaves 0/1 fates unchanged and does not annualize a cohort survival proportion. Survey durations differ among records, so these percentages are not comparable annual survival rates. Empty growth rows indicate unavailable measured live-area data, not a zero growth rate. Both panels are descriptive and show no inferential intervals. Legacy resampling intervals are retained in the survival-summary CSV only and do not estimate between-program uncertainty.
NOAA naturally occurring fragments are observations explicitly coded fragment = Y, not intact NOAA colonies or restoration outplants. In the source integration this designation uses interval-end Colony.Type AF/SF; all such observations therefore have a live endpoint. Their survival estimate is withheld as not estimable from these endpoint-selected records; the 94 observations remain in the inventory and growth summaries. Neely's AF code also denotes naturally attached fragments, not outplants. Neely records enter their separate row only for collector-verified AF at first sighting, followed subsequently as an origin cohort. BR/EN/remnant are not stable states or proof of nonfragment origin; the 1,966 other Neely intervals retain unknown physical fragment origin. Late appearance, site, size and fate do not define AF. The two initially loose fragments are retained with both deaths in the full inventory but excluded from the consistent attached-colony primary protocol and from the AF row. FUNDEMAR fragments were nursery-held on PVC tables, not reef outplants. Pausch records are nursery outplants; Kuffner records are nursery-reared fragments epoxied to removable discs and bolted to reef blocks, not a test of reattachment after breakage. The origin of USGS outplanted colonies is unspecified. Mendoza-Quiroz sexual cohorts are split between Puerto Morelos Nursery and Cuevones/Picudas reef outplants; they are not asexual fragments.
The subtype inventory contains 1,934 survival records from 7 studies; descriptive survival estimates use 1,840 records after withholding the endpoint-selected natural fragments. Live-area growth includes 1,271 records from 5 studies. Growth requires positive initial live area and observed live endpoints, available annual live-area change, and positive interval duration; this script makes no total-area substitution. Kuffner and Mendoza-Quiroz growth records lack the required live-area change field and do not enter panel b. The source integration treats surviving USGS colonies as having 100% live cover based on the photographs.
Subtype, study, site, handling, and sample size are not independently balanced; these summaries do not estimate treatment effects or rank restoration methods.
